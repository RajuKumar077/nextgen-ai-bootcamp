Ingredient 1 · Role

👨‍🍳 Who is cooking?

A pastry chef and a grill chef cook very differently.

The role sets vocabulary, depth and tone.

🧁 Pastry chef

"You are a polite vendor-relations manager."

🔥 Grill chef

"You are an SAP procurement analyst."

One line, big effect. We'll compare personas live in Main 3.

Ingredient 3 · Context

🧂 The house rules

The chef has never worked in your kitchen.

It doesn't know your thresholds, approvers or policies, unless you write them down.

House rules

₹5 lakh = ₹5,00,000

Above ₹5,00,000 → Finance Controller

Otherwise → AP team handles it

This is where SAP expertise matters most. Only you know the house rules.
Ingredient 4 · Format

🍱 How should it be plated?

The plate

– Vendor:

– Invoice number:

– PO number:

– Invoice amount:

– Issue (1 line):

– Escalate? (Yes/No + reason):

– Next action:

Same plate, every time.

Easy to scan, compare, and paste into a ticket.

Like a fixed ALV layout: same columns, every run
The most forgotten ingredient. The taste test will prove it.
Ingredient 5 · Rules

🚫 The allergies

🙅

No guessing

"If a value isn't in the email, write Not stated."

✂️

Keep it short

"Max 20 words per line."

🧾

Stay on the plate

"Don't add information that isn't in the email."

Remember Day 1? Rules are what stop the bluffing.

Kitchen tip
Spell out the house rules.
Numbers, thresholds, owners.

"₹5 lakh = ₹5,00,000. Above it → Finance Controller. Otherwise → AP team."

Like customising a config table, not mentioning it in the corridor
Especially important for small local models.

What the taste test tells us

Every ingredient has a job

🍱

Format = the salt

Leave it out and facts fall off the plate (3/5)

✂️

Rules = portion control

Without them: 69 words instead of ~45

🧂

Context = correctness

Facts can all be present and the decision still wrong (remember CELL 5)

An important nuance: the scorecard checks facts, not correctness. Context fixed the escalation decision.
Facts present ≠ decision correct.
